Rune-casting as a metaphor for reading business signals

Odin didn’t stumble onto the runes. He hung nine nights from Yggdrasil, wounded and fasting, until the symbols blazed into his mind. That’s the first lesson: foresight isn’t given—it’s earned through deliberate discomfort and attention. In business, we call that “monitoring signals,” but the principle is the same. You don’t wait for a dashboard to light up; you train yourself to see the patterns before they become crises.

Symbols as signals

When you cast runes, you’re not predicting the future; you’re reading a constellation of symbols that reflect forces already in motion. A single rune like Fehu (wealth) can mean abundance or loss, depending on its position and the runes around it. Business metrics work the same way. Revenue is a lagging indicator—it tells you what already happened. Customer churn, support ticket spikes, or even the tone of Slack threads are leading indicators. They’re the runes of your operation, whispering what’s coming if you’re sharp enough to listen.

Modern analytics tools give us the illusion of precision, but the runes remind us that interpretation is everything. A reversed Hagalaz (hail) doesn’t mean “disaster”; it means disruption, a force you can’t control but can prepare for. In business, that’s the supply chain hiccup or the quiet exodus of top talent. The signal is there; the question is whether you’ve built the intuition to act.

Leading vs. lagging: the runes of time

Most dashboards show you the past—last quarter’s MRR, last month’s churn. That’s like reading the runes from right to left, treating the future as a mirror of what’s already done. But the Norse didn’t see time as a straight line. The runes land where they land, and their order is a conversation, not a timeline. A Sowilo (sun) in the “past” position might signal a past win that’s still casting light on your present. In business, that’s the product launch that’s still driving referrals or the culture shift that’s quietly improving retention.

Leading indicators are the runes that land in the “now” but point forward. A sudden drop in demo requests? That’s a reversed Jera (harvest), warning that next quarter’s pipeline is at risk. The key is to treat these signals as part of a system, not isolated data points. Just as Odin didn’t interpret a single rune in isolation, you shouldn’t react to a single metric without context. Earning your foresight

Odin’s sacrifice on Yggdrasil wasn’t just about gaining knowledge; it was about proving he was ready to use it. Business foresight works the same way. You can’t just install a tool and expect it to tell you what’s next. You have to do the work: define which signals matter, set up the systems to track them, and—most importantly—build the discipline to act before the lagging indicators confirm your fears.

That’s where the mischief comes in. The runes don’t give answers; they give you the right questions. Is that dip in engagement a blip or a trend? Is that spike in support tickets a one-time issue or a systemic flaw? The answers aren’t in the data—they’re in how you choose to read it. And like Odin, you’ll only get better at it by doing it, by sitting with the discomfort of uncertainty until the patterns become clear.

  • Start small: Pick one leading indicator (e.g., time-to-first-reply in support) and track it for a month. Note how it correlates with lagging metrics like churn.
  • Map your runes: Assign a “rune” (symbol) to each key signal in your business. A rising graph for growth, a storm cloud for risk. Visualizing them this way forces you to interpret, not just report.
  • Trust your intuition: The runes don’t lie, but they don’t spell it out either. The same goes for your data. If something feels off, dig deeper—even if the dashboard says “green.”

Foresight isn’t about certainty; it’s about readiness. And readiness, like the runes, is something you earn.

“The wise man sees the storm before the clouds gather—
Odin hung from the tree; you’ll hang from the data.”
