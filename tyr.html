📖 Týr's Lore

The One-Handed Judge — Týr, the bravest of the gods, placed his hand in Fenrir's mouth as a pledge of good faith. When the gods betrayed the wolf and bound him, Týr's hand was bitten off — but he did not flinch. He is the god of law, justice, and the assembly's oaths. Týr's Code embeds that same honor into your agency: contract generation frameworks, compliance checklists that never sleep, and ethical guardrails so your automations serve your clients without overstepping.

Per Gylfaginning 25 and 34: Týr presides over lawsuits and justice, and lost his hand to Fenrir. (The "single-combat" framing is a later scholarly emphasis, not a primary epithet; "heroic sacrifice" is interpretive.)

“Týr's the runes of victory know I; I grave them on my son's sword-hilt; some on the whetstone, some on the war-board, and on the bear's paw of the bold.” Sigrdrífa's Spell (Sigrdrífmál, Larrington, tr.) — Týr's victory runes
The Business Lesson

💡 Honor lives at the trust boundary — keep a human in the loop

Týr is the only god who put his own hand in the wolf's mouth as a pledge of good faith. He paid for trust with his hand. The business lesson is sharp: at every trust boundary, automation must earn its mandate — and the highest-stakes actions stay human-approved.

Contracts, compliance gates, and client data are Týr's domain. Automate the drafting, the checklists, the monitoring — but never let a bot bind a client without a person's sign-off. Honor in automation means human approval at the points that matter.

Automate the paperwork, not the pledge: generate contracts and run compliance checks automatically, but keep a human approval at every trust boundary.
