📖 Hermóðr's Lore

War-Spirit — Hermóðr (Old Norse Hermóðr, "war-spirit") is named in Snorri's Gylfaginning as a son of Óðinn. When Baldr was slain and the Æsir mourned, Hermóðr volunteered to ride to the underworld to plead for Baldr's return. He leapt onto Sleipnir, Óðinn's eight-legged horse, and rode nine nights through deep, dark valleys until he came to the river Gjöll and the gate of Hel.

There Hel set the terms: Baldr would be released if all things, living and dead, wept for him. Hermóðr returned with Baldr's ring Draupnir as a token — but one giantess, Þökk (Loki in disguise), would not weep, and so Baldr remained in Hel until Ragnarök. Hermóðr is the original reliable messenger: he crossed the boundary, delivered the ask, and brought the confirmation back. (Note: he appears mainly in Snorri's account; some scholars read him as a literary device, but the ride itself is the attested episode — Gylfaginning 49.)
