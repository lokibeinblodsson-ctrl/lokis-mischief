📜 The Sagas & Eddas

the great Norse sources

Poetic Edda 13th c. MS

The Codex Regius — mythological and heroic poems: Völuspá (the Seeress's prophecy), Hávamál (Odin's words), Þrymskviða, and the lays of Sigurd.

Prose Edda Snorri, ~1220

Snorri Sturluson's handbook of skaldic metaphor: Gylfaginning (the tricking of Gylfi), Skáldskaparmál, Háttatal.

Völsunga saga legendary

The saga of the Völsung clan — Sigurd, Brynhild, the Rhinegold, and the dragon Fáfnir. Source for much of the Ring cycle.

Saga of the Ynglings euhemerist

Snorri's origin-myth of the Swedish kings, tracing them to the Æsir as mortal men remembered as gods.

Heimskringla kings' sagas

Snorri's collected sagas of the Norwegian kings — history written as story.

Gylfaginning within Prose Edda

The framing tale: a mortal king, Gylfi, visits Asgard disguised as Gangleri and is told the cosmology of the Nine Worlds.

Hávamál within Poetic Edda

"The Sayings of the High One" — Odin's gnomic wisdom: hospitality, self-reliance, and the rune-spell he won on the world-tree.

Völuspá within Poetic Edda

"The Seeress's Prophecy" — the creation of the world and its end at Ragnarök, spoken by a dead völva.
